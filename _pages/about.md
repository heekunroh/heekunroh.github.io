I am a systems engineer and PhD candidate in the Department of Aeronautics and Astronautics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT). My research focuses on systems engineering, complexity quantification, design processes, and space situational awareness. My doctoral thesis concerns complexity quantification and budgeting for engineering systems using graph-based abstraction.

I am advised by Professor Olivier L. de Weck through the MIT Engineering Systems Laboratory, and I collaborate with Professors Johannes J. Norheim and Edward F. Crawley. My current work includes methods for representing and measuring the complexity of engineering systems, optimizing multidisciplinary design sequences, and assessing the visibility of uncatalogued resident space objects using in-situ optical sensor constellations.

Before joining MIT, I was an appointed researcher in the Flight Dynamics and Control Lab at KAIST, where I worked on trajectory optimization, sequential convex optimization, and in-house optimizer development. I previously served as an associate systems engineer at Satrec Initiative, contributing to Earth-observation satellite attitude determination and control, onboard software development and testing, mission analysis, verification and validation, and satellite commissioning.

I earned an M.S. in Aerospace Engineering and a B.S. in Aerospace Engineering and Electrical Engineering from KAIST. I graduated summa cum laude and was a KAIST Presidential Fellow.
