My research integrates systems engineering, complexity science, design methodology, and space systems. I develop quantitative methods to represent, analyze, and manage the complexity of engineering systems and their design processes.

## Current Research — MIT (2023–Present)

- Complexity quantification and budgeting for engineering systems using graph-based abstraction
- Systems engineering theories and system-probing methods
- Design structure matrix analysis, spectral clustering, and controlled system decomposition
- Optimal design sequences for multidisciplinary analysis and optimization
- Visibility analysis and upper bounds for uncatalogued resident space objects using in-situ optical sensor constellations
- Space situational awareness and marketplace architecture

## Previous Research — KAIST (2022–2023)

- Trajectory optimization and sequential convex optimization
- Development of in-house quadratic programming and second-order cone programming solvers

## Previous Research — Satrec Initiative (2019–2022)

- Satellite attitude determination and control for Earth-observation missions
- Quaternion guidance with rate constraints
- Non-iterative attitude guidance on geodesic lines
- High-precision satellite attitude and misalignment estimation
- Onboard flight-control software development, testing, verification, and validation

## Previous Research — KAIST (2017–2019)

- Sequential convex programming for real-time guidance
- Trajectory optimization of aerodynamically controlled vehicles
- Impact-time and impact-angle control guidance
- Mixed-integer linear programming for optimal weapon-target assignment
- Bearings-only target tracking and angle-only estimation filters
